## In two sentences what is the client's real problem and how is it different from what they asked for

The client cannot reliably separate avoidable cutting loss from revision losses or tell whether existing nesting and offcut practices use material well. They asked for AI, but first need evidence on manual versus Strumis automatic nesting and a workable process within project-specific budgets.

## Who are the people in this deal and what does each need to hear

- **Cost Control:** a forwardable update this week, verified savings and a loss breakdown they can defend.
- **Nesting planner:** they keep approval authority and help shape the trial.
- **Yard:** stock checks and offcut tracking must be quick and workable.
- **Engineering and quality:** correct revisions and traceability; distinguish internal errors from client-change losses that may be claimed back.
- **Production and operators:** existing cutting controls stay in place, and delivery dates matter as much as yield.
- **Management and Finance:** capped spend, fixed project budgets and approved transfer costing. Extra trailer hire is a separate concern to investigate using existing data; the budget owner is still unconfirmed.
- **IT:** we start with approved exports and limited access.

## The five questions for the next call ranked with why they matter and assumptions

1. **Can the operating teams walk us through a recent job, and who approves the pilot?** We need to validate the problem beyond Cost Control. I assume the contact can bring the right people together; ownership is unconfirmed.
2. **Can planners reproduce a case where Strumis automatic nesting loses to manual?** This tests the central claim before building. I assume neither method wins until inputs, settings and machine constraints are matched.
3. **How is waste measured, including revision losses and reusable returns?** This determines avoidable cost. I treat the rates as provisional and 80,000 tonnes as capacity, not actual throughput; claims are tracked separately.
4. **Can Finance approve sharing stock, and how would both project budgets change? Can Yard retrieve it?** Accounting and access can block reuse. I assume project allocations stay separate unless an approved, traceable transfer is possible.
5. **Which released jobs can provide 20 batches despite holds and revisions?** This protects delivery and comparison quality. I assume one plate line and two projects, with Engineering confirming the current release; otherwise the trial window extends.

## Which options did you consider and why did you pick this one

I considered process improvements, configuring Strumis, a small stock-matching tool and a custom nesting platform. I chose a fair test of the automatic option they already own, adding a tool only for a proven gap. I ruled out custom AI, autonomous cutting and immediate cross-project pooling. Trailer utilisation is a credible next option using existing CarTrack and booking records, but low utilisation alone does not prove unnecessary hire. We deliver the pilot ourselves, not just recommend packages.

## How will the client know the pilot worked

Cost Control establishes the baseline from at least 10 reconciled batches. On 20 distinct batches, compare A: manual, B: existing automatic and, if needed, C: configured automatic or improved stock selection, using identical inputs and time budgets. Execute at least five approved candidate batches. Success is at least 3% lower aggregate material cost per accepted tonne than A, no added planning/handling time or delays, and positive actual savings after operating costs. Report C's incremental benefit over B separately. The planner logs plans, Yard verifies stock and Production records actuals. Stop expansion below the threshold or with unreliable data; pause immediately for revision, material, traceability or safety failures.

## What does this cost the client and us and what value does the client get

**Price:** $35,000 fixed-fee pilot: $10,500 at kickoff, $14,000 after the week-two review and approval to continue, and $10,500 on the final report. A failed week-two gate ends the work with only $10,500 payable. Optional support is $1,000/month; travel, tax, new CAM licences and wider rollout are excluded.

**Delivery cost:** 18 FDE days × $700 + 12 engineer days × $600 + 4 specialist days × $800 + $1,000 tools + $2,000 contingency = **$26,000**. Estimated contribution is **$9,000** before overhead and tax. Client involvement is about 60 staff-hours.

**Illustrative value:** 10,000 eligible input tonnes/year × 2% avoided purchases × ($900 purchase price − $300 scrap recovery) = **$120,000/year**. Less $10,000 extra handling/cutting and $12,000 support leaves **$98,000/year**, or **$63,000 after the pilot fee** in a full operating year. All figures are USD assumptions; actual prices, volumes and trial results replace them before rollout. This is not a guaranteed saving.

## Where did you push back narrow the ask or tell the client not to do something

I narrowed the broad AI request to one plate line and two projects with separate stock allocations. They already own an automatic nesting option and already check offcuts, so I would test those processes before building replacements. I would not promise cross-project sharing without Finance approval or count claimable revision losses as nesting savings.

## What is most likely to go wrong and what did you not trust

The most likely failure is an apparently available offcut that is buried, reserved for another project or tied to a job whose revision changed. Require physical retrieval and release checks. I would verify the claim that manual nesting beats automatic, the waste-rate definitions and the inconsistent annual/monthly capacity figures. The contact explicitly says they see nesting from outside; their view needs checking with the operating teams.

## What did you use AI for

I used Gemini Deepsearch for the first drafts and revisions against the brief, then Codex for review, arithmetic, editing and a final check against the discovery notes. AI helped structure the proposal, but the drafts included unsupported technical assumptions and conflicting commercial figures. The first review also missed confirmed details because the call notes had not yet been supplied. I discarded the large custom-build proposal and unverified machine details; the final version tests the existing Strumis option and respects project costing.
